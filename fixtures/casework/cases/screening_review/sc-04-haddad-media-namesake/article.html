Limassol freight broker charged in customs bribery probe

Prosecutors say cash payments let undeclared containers leave the port without inspection
By Nikos Andreou, Courts Reporter · Published 21 October 2026, 18:40
Containers stacked at the port of Limassol. File photo.

LIMASSOL - A freight broker who has worked at Limassol port for more than two decades was charged on Tuesday with paying bribes to customs officers so that undeclared shipments could clear the port, according to court documents seen by the Courier.

Karim Haddad, 61, the founder of Haddad Freight Services, appeared before the district court alongside two serving customs officers. Prosecutors allege that between 2022 and 2025 he made cash payments of about EUR 180,000 so that containers of cigarettes and electronics left the port without inspection.

Haddad, a Cypriot national who lives in Limassol, Cyprus, did not enter a plea. His lawyer told reporters that his client denies any wrongdoing and will defend himself.

The court released him on bail of EUR 50,000 and ordered him to surrender his travel documents. The case returns to court on 14 January.

The customs authority said it had suspended the two officers and opened an internal review of clearance procedures at the port.
