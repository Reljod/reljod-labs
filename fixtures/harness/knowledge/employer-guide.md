# Employer Guide: Remitting Contributions

Harbour Bank & Trust Pension Plan, Employer Guide, edition of 1 February 2024. SPECIMEN, a fictional bank. Illustrative, not legal advice: the National Pensions Act (2024 Revision) is the authority.

## Who must be enrolled

Every employee aged 18 up to the normal age of pension entitlement (65) must be a member of a pension plan, part-time employees included (National Pensions Act s.4(1), s.25(1)). A Caymanian or permanent resident is pensionable from the first day. An employee who is neither Caymanian nor a permanent resident need not be enrolled until they have worked in the Islands for a continuous period of more than nine months (s.25(2)(a)). The nine months need not be with one employer.

Three groups are outside mandatory coverage: Caymanians under 23 in full-time education, non-Caymanian household domestics, and non-Caymanians inside the nine-month period.

Send each new member the enrolment information within 21 days of the employee becoming eligible (s.20).

## Contribution rates

Each month the employer pays 10% of each employee's earnings into the plan and may deduct up to 5% of earnings from the employee. The standard split is 5% from the employer and 5% from the employee (s.47(3)). An employer may pay more than 5%, in which case the employee's share falls so the total stays 10%. An employer may not recover its own share from the employee, and may not pay the contribution to the employee instead of the plan.

Mandatory contributions apply only to earnings up to the Year's Maximum Pensionable Earnings (YMPE): CI$87,000 per year. Earnings above the YMPE are treated as additional voluntary contributions. There is no basic exemption: contributions apply from the first dollar of earnings.

Earnings include wages, salary, leave pay, fees, commissions, gratuities and certain bonuses. They exclude severance payments, normal pension entitlement payments, long service recognition payments and health insurance premiums (s.3, "earnings").

## When contributions are due

Contributions for a month in which the employee worked must reach the plan on or before the 15th day of the following month, whatever the pay period (s.48(2)-(3)). Contributions for April are due by 15 May. The deadline is close of business on the 15th.

Send the contribution Schedule with the payment: one line per member, with the member number, earnings, employer contribution and employee contribution. Show pension contributions separately from other payments such as health insurance.

## If a contribution is late

A contribution not received by close of business on the 15th is a delinquent contribution from the next day (s.48(3)). The employer owes interest at the current Cayman prime rate plus five percentage points, calculated daily from the day after the deadline (s.50). The administrator will contact the employer at once to collect, and keeps a record of each effort. A delinquent contribution still unpaid on the 15th of the following month is reported in writing to the Director of Labour and Pensions by the 20th, and the plan then tells the affected employees. See the Delinquency Procedure.

Employer contributions that are due and unpaid are held on trust, and the administrator has a lien on the employer's assets for them (s.49).

## Records

Keep payroll and pension records for at least five years from the date prepared: names and contact details, start date, full or part time, pay rate, gross and net pay, bonuses, terminations, deductions, contributions with proof of payment, periods and payment dates, and any interest paid (s.18A).
